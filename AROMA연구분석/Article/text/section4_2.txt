As shown in Tables 6 and 7, the two datasets exhibit contrasting augmentation responses: AITeX demonstrates substantial AROMA advantage, whereas Kolektor converges across methods near ceiling performance.

On AITeX (Table 6), AROMA achieves 0.4533 mean mAP@0.5, outperforming both Baseline (0.3591, +26% relative gain) and Random (0.4249, +7% relative gain). The improvement is robust across seeds: both augmented methods raise the mean while reducing seed variance (Baseline std 0.0426 versus 0.0261/0.0300 for Random/AROMA). AROMA's edge over Random is modest and consistent in direction across all three seeds, though not statistically significant at n = 3: AROMA's tighter confidence interval and lower variance suggest more uniform placement quality across the tiled defect space (Figure 4.2-1). The single-class, morphologically homogeneous nature of AITeX—combined with its tiled repetitive structure—appears amenable to context-aware placement: AROMA's ability to identify and prioritize compatible background contexts within the tile geometry yields measurable gains.

On Kolektor (Table 7), baseline performance is already near-ceiling (0.9503 mAP@0.5); synthetic augmentation provides only marginal gains. Random achieves 0.9837 (+3.34 pp), and AROMA achieves 0.9866 (+3.63 pp vs. Baseline, +0.29 pp vs. Random). At this ceiling level, both placement strategies converge (Figure 4.2-2): the 36-image Kolektor training set, combined with high intrinsic dataset quality and balanced defect representation, leaves little headroom for contextual placement to improve detection. The baseline's seed-level spread (std 0.0398, seed 2 reaching 0.995) reveals that small-dataset randomness can dominate augmentation effects; both augmented methods narrow this spread (AROMA std 0.0080). AROMA's performance parity with Random—despite its compatibility-aware design—indicates that in near-ceiling, low-headroom regimes (Kolektor; see also MTD in Section 4.3), the placement policy contributes less than baseline dataset characteristics.

The contrast between AITeX and Kolektor validates the conditional nature of AROMA's benefit: meaningful gains emerge when baseline headroom exists (AITeX: low baseline, high improvement potential) and the defect-background compatibility structure is informative (AITeX: diverse tile contexts; Kolektor: already well-covered by random placement in a small, balanced set). No claim of universal improvement is asserted; instead, the results demonstrate that AROMA recovers value in constrained regimes where random placement leaves coverage gaps, while remaining neutral in high-headroom or data-rich scenarios.
