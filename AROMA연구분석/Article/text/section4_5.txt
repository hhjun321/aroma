##4.5. Sensitivity of the Fixed Design Choices

Beyond the per-dataset parameters that AROMA estimates from data (Sections 3.2.2–3.2.3), the pipeline retains two dataset-independent conventions that are fixed by design: the weights of the ROI score (Eq. 2, 0.5/0.3/0.2) and the percentile positions that define the tertile partitions (P33/P66). This section verifies that neither functions as a hidden tuning knob.

Every comparison follows the same controlled procedure. For each dataset, the full ROI candidate pool, the compatibility model (ctx_prior), the cluster priors (morph_prior), the selection budget K, and the allocation procedure of Section 3.2.4 (pair quotas, class floors, and the source-diversity cap) are all held fixed; only the constant under test is changed. Each perturbed configuration then (i) recomputes every candidate's ROI_score — for weight perturbations by reweighting the three terms, for boundary perturbations by re-deriving the subtype labels and hence the quality term — (ii) re-runs the unchanged allocation procedure on the re-scored pool, and (iii) compares the resulting top-K selection with that of the reference configuration, reported as the retention (the fraction of the reference selection that survives). Any difference is therefore attributable solely to the perturbed constant. As a harness check, re-scoring under the adopted configuration itself reproduces the pipeline's production selection exactly. The analysis operates at the selection level rather than re-training a detector per configuration: the selection is the only channel through which these constants can influence the training data, re-training the full grid (66+ configurations × 5 datasets × 3 seeds) would be prohibitive, and the link from selection to downstream mAP is measured independently by the ablation of Section 4.4, which also calibrates the downstream scale of selection changes.

**Weight sensitivity (Eq. 2).** The weight simplex was swept exhaustively in steps of 0.1 (66 configurations) on all five datasets, comparing each configuration's top-K selection against that of the adopted weights (0.5/0.3/0.2). Table 12 summarizes representative configurations spanning both regimes. Throughout the interior of the simplex the selection is stable: every configuration within ±0.1 of the adopted weights retains 95.5–100% of the selection (per-dataset means 98.5–100%). The consequential departures are the three component-removal configurations. Dropping the quality term (0.6/0.4/0.0) retains only 53.5–64.8% of the selection on four of the five datasets; a context-only score behaves similarly (49.5–65.5%); and a morphology-only score degrades furthest, falling to 34.0% on Kolektor. No component-removal configuration is stable across all five datasets, although the failure mode is dataset-dependent: Kolektor tolerates quality removal (94.5%) but collapses without the context term, whereas MVTec Leather is the most sensitive row-wise, losing roughly half the selection under any removal. The contrast between the two regimes is the finding: the selection is insensitive to the exact weight values and sensitive only to which terms participate. The specific values 0.5/0.3/0.2 therefore encode a priority order (context > morphology > quality), not a tuned optimum.

**Tertile-boundary sensitivity (P33/P66).** The percentile positions defining the subtype tertiles were perturbed by ±5 percentile points, individually and jointly (P33 → 28–38, P66 → 61–71, eight perturbed configurations per dataset), with the per-dataset degeneracy safeguard of Section 3.2.3 applied unchanged at the perturbed positions. Table 13 reports the resulting subtype reassignment and selection retention. The response is proportional and exhibits no instability cliff: shifting a single boundary by ±5 points relabels 3.1–9.1% of defects — the order of the distribution mass moved by the shift itself — and retains 67.5–98.0% of the top-K selection; shifting both boundaries simultaneously relabels 7.7–14.5% and retains 64.0–94.5%. The influence path is structurally bounded: a subtype reassignment can change only the quality term, which is weighted 0.2 and takes discrete values, so a single relabeling shifts a candidate's score by at most 0.12.

Together with the ablation of Section 4.4, these results locate the source of AROMA's effect precisely: downstream gains require the presence of each scoring mechanism (removing a stage or a score component changes the outcome), while the numerical constants configuring those mechanisms can be varied within a broad neighborhood without materially changing what is selected. Section 4.4 also calibrates the downstream stakes of these membership changes: replacing the compatibility-scored selection with a fully random one — a 100% membership change — costs 3.8 pp of mAP on Severstal (Table 11), whereas the alternative weight configurations of Table 12 leave 53–95% of the selection in place. The exact weight values therefore operate far from the regime where selection changes translate into material downstream differences.
