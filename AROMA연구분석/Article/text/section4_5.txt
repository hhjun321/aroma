##4.5. Sensitivity of the Tertile Partition Boundaries

With the ROI score of Eq. (2) reduced to an unweighted combination, the only dataset-independent convention retained by the pipeline is the use of the P33/P66 percentile positions to define the tertile partitions (Sections 3.2.2–3.2.3). This section verifies that these fixed percentile positions do not act as a hidden tuning knob.

The subtype labels of Section 3.2.3 serve interpretation and reporting; the ROI ranking of Eq. (2) does not consume them, so a boundary shift cannot alter which placements are generated. The remaining question is whether the labels themselves are stable. The percentile positions were perturbed by ±5 percentile points, individually and jointly (P33 → 28–38, P66 → 61–71, eight perturbed configurations per dataset), with the per-dataset degeneracy safeguard of Section 3.2.3 applied unchanged at the perturbed positions. The response is proportional and exhibits no instability cliff: a single-boundary shift relabels 3.1–9.1% of defects and a joint shift relabels 7.7–14.5% (Table 13) — in each case the order of the distribution mass moved by the shift itself, not an amplification of it.

**Table 13.** Stability of the subtype labels under tertile-boundary perturbation (P33/P66 shifted by ±5 percentile points). Ranges over the perturbed configurations of each group.

| Perturbation | Subtype reassignment |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Single boundary ±5 pt | 3.1–9.1% |
| Both boundaries ±5 pt | 7.7–14.5% |

Together with the ablation of Section 4.4, these results identify the source of AROMA's effect: downstream gains depend on the context-compatibility mechanism — removing a placement stage changes the outcome, and replacing the compatibility-scored ROI selection with a random one reduces Severstal mAP by 3.8 pp — whereas the only remaining fixed convention, the tertile boundaries, cannot affect placement and causes only proportional changes in the reported labels.
