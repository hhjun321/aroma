##4.5. Sensitivity of the Fixed Design Choices

Beyond the per-dataset parameters that AROMA estimates from data (Sections 3.2.2–3.2.3), the pipeline retains two dataset-independent conventions that are fixed by design: the weights of the ROI score (Eq. 2, 0.5/0.3/0.2) and the percentile positions that define the tertile partitions (P33/P66). This section verifies that neither functions as a hidden tuning knob. Both analyses operate at the selection level: the full candidate pool of each dataset is re-scored under a perturbed configuration, the production allocation procedure of Section 3.2.4 is re-run unchanged, and the resulting top-K selection is compared with the adopted configuration's selection (overlap fraction). The selection is the only channel through which these constants can influence training data, and the link from selection to downstream mAP is measured independently in Section 4.4.

**Weight sensitivity (Eq. 2).** Two sweeps were run on all five datasets: an exhaustive sweep of the weight simplex in steps of 0.1 (66 configurations), compared against the adopted weights (0.5/0.3/0.2), and a ratio sweep within the quality-free plane, compared against the two-term configuration (0.6 · ctx_prior + 0.4 · morph_prior). Table 12 summarizes both, and together they separate weight changes into two regimes. Changing the values of participating terms is inconsequential: every configuration within ±0.1 of the adopted weights retains 95.5–100% of the selection (per-dataset means 98.5–100%), and within the quality-free plane even inverting the ratio from 0.9/0.1 to 0.1/0.9 retains 87.5–100% of the two-term selection — no ratio in that plane is an operative choice. Changing which terms participate is consequential: dropping the quality term (0.6/0.4/0.0) retains only 53.5–64.8% of the adopted selection on four of the five datasets, a context-only score behaves similarly (49.5–65.5%), and a morphology-only score degrades furthest, falling to 34.0% on Kolektor. The failure mode is dataset-dependent — Kolektor tolerates quality removal (94.5%) but collapses without the context term, whereas MVTec Leather loses roughly half the selection under any removal — but the aggregate conclusion is uniform: the selection is insensitive to the exact weight values and sensitive only to which terms participate. The specific values 0.5/0.3/0.2 therefore encode a priority order (context > morphology > quality), not a tuned optimum.

**Tertile-boundary sensitivity (P33/P66).** The percentile positions defining the subtype tertiles were perturbed by ±5 percentile points, individually and jointly (P33 → 28–38, P66 → 61–71, eight perturbed configurations per dataset), with the per-dataset degeneracy safeguard of Section 3.2.3 applied unchanged at the perturbed positions. Table 13 reports the resulting subtype reassignment and selection retention. The response is proportional and exhibits no instability cliff: shifting a single boundary by ±5 points relabels 3.1–9.1% of defects — the order of the distribution mass moved by the shift itself — and retains 67.5–98.0% of the top-K selection; shifting both boundaries simultaneously relabels 7.7–14.5% and retains 64.0–94.5%. The influence path is structurally bounded: a subtype reassignment can change only the quality term, which is weighted 0.2 and takes discrete values, so a single relabeling shifts a candidate's score by at most 0.12.

| Perturbation | Subtype reassignment | Top-K retention |
|--------------|----------------------|-----------------|

Together with the ablation of Section 4.4, these results locate the source of AROMA's effect precisely: downstream gains require the presence of each scoring mechanism (removing a stage or a score component changes the outcome), while the numerical constants configuring those mechanisms can be varied within a broad neighborhood without materially changing what is selected. Section 4.4 also calibrates the downstream stakes of these membership changes: replacing the compatibility-scored selection with a fully random one — a 100% membership change — costs 3.8 pp of mAP on Severstal (Table 11), whereas the alternative weight configurations of Table 12 leave 53–95% of the selection in place. The exact weight values therefore operate far from the regime where selection changes translate into material downstream differences.
